# Drip 006 — Training Volume
Source: ISSA CPT, Ch.9 — Acute variables: training volume & density. Send: Thursday.

**Subject:** The number that runs your progress

---

You can't manage what you don't measure. Here's the number that matters most: **training volume**.

Volume is the total work you do, and the formula is simple:

**Sets × Reps × Load.**

Three sets of 10 reps at 100 lbs is 3,000 lbs of volume. Bump to three sets of 12 and you're at 3,600 — more work, more stimulus, without touching the weight. This is what progressive overload looks like in actual numbers, and it's why "I did chest today" tells you nothing while "I beat last week's volume" tells you everything.

There's a sibling number too: **density** — the same volume done in less time. Cut your rest, finish the same work faster, and you've overloaded a different way.

**Do this week:** For one exercise, do the math. Sets × reps × load, this week. Write it down. Next week, make that number go up — by adding a rep, a set, a little weight, or finishing the same work with less rest.
