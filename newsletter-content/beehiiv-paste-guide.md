# Brains & Gains — Beehiiv Formatting & Paste Guide

Beehiiv uses a **block editor**, not markdown. If you paste `##` or `**` they show up as literal characters. So: paste the plain words, then apply the same light formatting recipe every time. The drips are short — this is ~2 minutes per post once you've done one.

---

## The three fields (don't mix them up)
When you build a post, Beehiiv asks for three separate text fields:

| Beehiiv field | Where you set it | What to paste |
|---|---|---|
| **Post Title** | The big field at the top of the editor | The short **TITLE** below (e.g. "Progressive Overload"). This is the web-archive headline and the *default* subject. |
| **Email Subject Line** | The "Email" / review step before sending | The **SUBJECT** below (the hook). Override the default here. |
| **Preview Text** | Same email step, under the subject | The **PREVIEW** below (one line). |

The body goes in the editor canvas underneath the title.

---

## The house style (same every week)
Build each drip body as these blocks, top to bottom:

1. **Text** — the intro line(s).
2. **Text** — the concept paragraph. **Bold the key term** (the name of the principle).
3. **Divider** — type `---` on a blank line and hit enter, or `/` → Divider.
4. **Text** — `Do this week:` in **bold**, then the action on the same line.
5. **Text** — the closing teaser line, in *italic*.
6. *(Every 3rd issue only)* **Button** — `/` → Button → label **Apply for coaching**, link `https://ajmfit.com/apply`.

### How to apply formatting after pasting
- **Bold / italic:** highlight the text → a small toolbar pops up → click **B** or *i*. (Or, while typing fresh, `**word**` bolds and `*word*` italicizes live.)
- **Heading:** highlight a line → toolbar → Heading. (None needed for these drips — they're short enough to skip headings.)
- **Divider / Button / Image:** type `/` on a new line to open the block menu.

### Send settings (same every post)
- Audience: **All subscribers** (free list).
- Schedule: **Thursday**, fixed time (e.g. 6:00 AM). Set it and never move it.

**SUBJECT:** The one rule that builds everything
**PREVIEW:** Your first drip from the ISSA cert — the principle the rest sits on.

**BODY:**
Welcome to Brains & Gains. Every Thursday I'll hand you one idea from the ISSA certification — small enough to read in a minute, useful enough to keep. We start with the principle the whole thing is built on.

Progressive overload. *(bold "Progressive overload")* Straight from the cert: for fitness to progress, the body must be forced to adapt to a stress greater than what it normally encounters. To build a stronger biceps, you have to lift a resistance it isn't used to. Lift the same weight for the same reps every week and you've given your body no reason to change — it already handles that. That's why people stall while still working hard. They're repeating workouts, not progressing them. The key word is incremental — small, steady increases, not heroics.

### Thursday 2 — Drip 002
**TITLE:** The General Adaptation Syndrome
**SUBJECT:** Why week one feels like nothing
**PREVIEW:** The reason a new program seems to "do nothing" at first.

**BODY:**
Last week: progressive overload. This week: why it takes patience.

The ISSA cert teaches the General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS) *(bold "General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS)")* — how your body responds to training stress in three stages. Alarm (first 2–3 weeks): fatigue, weakness, soreness; the early strength you gain is mostly your nervous system learning the movement, not muscle yet. Resistance (around 4–6 weeks): the real changes arrive — muscle size and strength. Exhaustion: what happens if the stress never lets up and you never recover — that's overtraining. Here's the part worth tattooing on your brain: after a hard enough session, fitness actually dips for a bit, then your body supercompensates, rebuilding to a higher level than before. That rebound is the whole point — but it runs on weeks, not days.

[divider]

Do this week: *(bold)* Don't judge a new program by how you feel on day four. Give any change a full 4–6 weeks before you decide it's working.

*Next Thursday: why the type of training you pick decides the results you get.* *(italic)*

### Thursday 3 — Drip 003
**TITLE:** Specificity
**SUBJECT:** Train for the thing you actually want
**PREVIEW:** No accidental six-pack from a marathon plan.

**BODY:**
The ISSA principle of specificity *(bold "principle of specificity")* is short and unforgiving: the adaptations you get are based on the type of training you do. Train for endurance, you get endurance. Train for max strength, you get strength. There's no accidental six-pack from a marathon plan, and no surprise marathon time from heavy triples. Your body adapts to exactly the demand you place on it — no more, no less. Most people train generally and hope specifically. They do a bit of everything, then wonder why nothing moves. The fix isn't more effort. It's aim. The cert also says to start where you actually are: begin with training that fits your current level, then progress as fitness develops.

[divider]

Do this week: *(bold)* Write your single main goal in one sentence — "get stronger," "build my chest," "run a 5K without stopping." Then look at last week's training and ask: did it actually look like that goal? Cut one thing that didn't belong.

*Next Thursday: why consistency beats intensity every time.* *(italic)*

### Thursday 4 — Drip 004  *(add the Apply button on this one)*
**TITLE:** Reversibility
**SUBJECT:** Use it or lose it (literally)
**PREVIEW:** The low bar to keep what you've built.

**BODY:**
The ISSA principle of reversibility *(bold "principle of reversibility")* is the one nobody wants to hear: you lose the effects of training after you stop. The cert calls the slide detraining, and it sets in after about two weeks off. This is why the person who trains at 70% every single week quietly buries the one who goes 100% for ten days and then vanishes. Adaptation is a tenant, not an owner — stop paying rent and it moves out. It's also good news: the bar to hold your progress is far lower than the bar to build it. A couple of short, honest sessions a week is enough to keep what you've earned, even in a busy stretch.

[divider]

Do this week: *(bold)* Look at your next 7 days and protect two non-negotiable sessions — even 25 minutes counts. The goal isn't a perfect week. It's not handing back what you've already built.

### Thursday 5 — Drip 005
**TITLE:** Individual Differences & Diminishing Returns
**SUBJECT:** Why his program won't work for you
**PREVIEW:** Two ISSA principles, one lesson: stop copying.

**BODY:**
Two ISSA principles, one lesson: stop copying.

Individual differences *(bold)* — there's no single way to train every person, because every person is unique: age, history, recovery, schedule, injuries, starting point. The program that transformed your friend was built for their variables, not yours. Borrow the principles; don't xerox the plan. Diminishing returns *(bold)* — everyone has a genetic ceiling. The effort that bought huge results in your first year buys smaller ones in your fifth. That's not failure, it's physics. The beginner adding 50 lbs to a lift in months and the veteran fighting for 5 lbs in a year can be training equally hard. Put together: compare yourself to last week's you, not to someone whose ceiling and timeline you can't see.

[divider]

Do this week: *(bold)* Drop one comparison. Unfollow the account, or just stop measuring against that one person. Replace it with your own logbook — what you lifted last week versus this week.

*Next Thursday: the simplest way to measure how much work you're actually doing.* *(italic)*

### Thursday 6 — Drip 006
**TITLE:** Training Volume
**SUBJECT:** The number that runs your progress
**PREVIEW:** Sets × reps × load — the metric that actually tells you something.

**BODY:**
You can't manage what you don't measure. The ISSA cert gives you the number that matters most: training volume. *(bold "training volume")* Volume is the total work you do, and the formula is simple: Sets × Reps × Load. *(bold "Sets × Reps × Load")* Three sets of 10 reps at 100 lbs is 3,000 lbs of volume. Bump to three sets of 12 and you're at 3,600 — more work, more stimulus, without touching the weight. This is what progressive overload looks like in actual numbers, and it's why "I did chest today" tells you nothing while "I beat last week's volume" tells you everything. There's a sibling number too: density — the same volume done in less time. Cut your rest, finish the same work faster, and you've overloaded a different way.

[divider]

Do this week: *(bold)* For one exercise, do the math — sets × reps × load, this week. Write it down. Next week, make that number go up: add a rep, a set, a little weight, or finish the same work with less rest.

*That wraps Module 1 — the principles. Next Thursday we start Module 2: dialing in the variables, beginning with intensity.* *(italic)*
